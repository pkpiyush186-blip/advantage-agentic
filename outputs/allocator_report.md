# Allocator Results - Discounted Thompson Sampling

Generated by `scripts/03_run_allocator.py`.

## 1. Method

- Hyperparameters swept over 80 configurations on **8 separate training worlds** (seeds 101-108). The demo world used for every reported result never picks a hyperparameter.
- Each configuration was scored on two axes: gain in conversions per rupee, and straight-through processing rate.
- Final evaluation on the demo world across 5 allocator seeds; the headline is the mean with the spread reported.
- Every run replays the same frozen world, the same noise stream and the same total spend, so conversions are directly comparable.

> **Finding, not a caveat.** gain-max: discount=0, full discounting - the estimate uses last week alone; balanced: discount=0, full discounting - the estimate uses last week alone. The discount grid spans its whole domain [0, 1], so this is the limit of the parameter rather than the limit of the search. It says something about the world: because channel advantage rotates, evidence older than about a week is not just less useful, it is actively misleading, and the best estimator in this world is close to memoryless. A longer memory is what the As-Is monthly manager effectively has, and it is precisely why the As-Is gains almost nothing over a frozen split.

## 3. The trade-off the proposal did not anticipate

The proposal set two targets that pull against each other: +8-12% on conversions per rupee (O2) and 40-60% straight-through processing (Section 15). They cannot both be maximised. An allocator that leans harder into the leading channel earns more conversions, but it crosses the 5 pp auto-execute threshold and gets trimmed by the 15 pp weekly cap more often - and both of those force a manager approval under the rulebook.

At the gain-max operating point, straight-through processing is **1%** (0 of 26 cycles ran untouched), against the 40-60% target.

Highest-gain configuration with straight-through processing at or above the 40% target: `concentration = 0.75`, `discount = 0`, giving +5.6% with 42% straight-through processing.

This is a decision for the marketing manager, not for the model: pay for more conversions with more of their own review time, or accept a smaller gain for a process that mostly runs itself. `outputs/fig_tradeoff.png` plots every configuration on both axes so the choice can be made with the numbers in view. Presenting that choice is a better answer than presenting one tuned number.

## 4. How much of the gain is Thompson Sampling?

The ablation uses the identical model and discounting but acts on the posterior *mean* - no sampling, so no exploration. It gains +9.1%, against +9.1% for Thompson Sampling.

So exploration contributes essentially nothing (-0.0 pp) and **the gain comes from recency-weighted estimation**, not from the bandit's exploration. This is the expected result at this data volume: around 500 conversions per channel per week makes the posterior very tight (see `fig_learning.png`), so a Thompson draw lands almost on top of the mean and there is little real uncertainty left to probe.

Two things follow, and both are worth saying before an examiner says them:

1. The honest claim for this project is that *fast, principled, recency-weighted re-estimation plus a disciplined projection onto the policy set* produces the gain. Thompson Sampling is the formulation used to get there, and it costs nothing, but it is not where the value comes from here.
2. Exploration would matter much more for a smaller advertiser, a newly launched channel with no history, or a weekly cycle with tens rather than hundreds of conversions. The 15% per-channel floor in the rulebook is also doing part of the exploration job already, by guaranteeing every channel keeps producing data.
